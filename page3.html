Fish and Game Regulations

Before heading out to catch Dungeness crab along the Northern California coast, it is important to understand the regulations established by the California Department of Fish and Wildlife (CDFW). These rules help protect crab populations, reduce accidental wildlife entanglements, and ensure that recreational crabbers are fishing responsibly. For the 2026–2027 season, the general recreational Dungeness crab limit is 10 crabs per day, with a minimum size requirement of 5¾ inches. The season is scheduled to run from November 7, 2026, through June 30, 2027, in most California counties, and through July 30, 2027, in Del Norte, Humboldt, and Mendocino counties. Dungeness crab cannot be harvested from San Francisco Bay or San Pablo Bay. However, regulations can change during the season. As of November 2026, CDFW has temporarily prohibited recreational crab traps in California Fishing Zones 1–5 due to whale and sea turtle entanglement concerns. Hoop nets and crab snares remain permitted, subject to their applicable requirements. CDFW has also delayed the recreational season in a portion of northern California because of elevated domoic acid levels. Crabbers should check current season openings, gear restrictions, health advisories, and marine protected area boundaries before every trip. If using crab traps when they are permitted, a valid Recreational Crab Trap Validation is required. View California Crab Regulations
