Target Species and Patterns

Dungeness crab (Metacarcinus magister) is one of the most sought-after species along the Northern California coast. Found in coastal waters, bays, and estuaries, Dungeness crab supports an important recreational and commercial fishery. During the winter months, crabbing activity typically increases as the season opens in many Northern California areas, creating opportunities for anglers fishing from shore, piers, and boats. Dungeness crab follow seasonal patterns influenced by water temperature, breeding cycles, molting, and migration. They can move between deeper offshore waters and shallower coastal areas, making location and timing important when setting traps or hoop nets. Bait selection, water depth, tides, and changing ocean conditions can all influence your chances of success. When targeting Dungeness crab, identification and size limits are especially important. California regulations generally require harvested Dungeness crab to measure at least 5¾ inches across the shell, measured in a straight line at the widest part.
